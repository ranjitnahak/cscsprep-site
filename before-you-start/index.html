BEFORE YOU START

Five things that quietly sink CSCS prep. None of them are about intelligence.

If you spot yourself in one of these, good. Every one is fixable.

01

I'll just read and see how it goes.

THE THOUGHT

I'll read a chapter here, watch a video there. I'll get through it eventually.

THE TRUTH

Most people preparing for the CSCS don't have a step-by-step process. They just have a book and good intentions.

There are 26 chapters. With no order, you read what feels comfortable and skip what feels scary. Then the exam asks about the part you skipped.

THE FIX

Build a step-by-step process before you build the habit.

  • Step 1: Fix your exam date
  • Step 2: Count the weeks between now and then
  • Step 3: Divide the 26 chapters across those weeks
  • Step 4: End every week with questions on what you covered

Now every study session has one question to answer: what's next? You never have to decide it again.

You don't need more effort. You need to know what comes next.

02

I'll start Monday.

THE THOUGHT

Once work calms down. Once I've got the book. Once I've got a clear 3 hours.

THE TRUTH

Monday isn't coming. When it arrives, it'll be another "not yet."

You're not waiting for time. You're waiting to feel ready, and that feeling only shows up after you begin.

THE FIX

Start tonight, and keep it small.

  • Set a 20-minute timer
  • Study anything CSCS
  • When it ends, stop

That's it. Twenty minutes tonight beats three perfect hours on Monday.

You wouldn't tell an athlete to wait till Monday for the first rep. Do yours tonight.

Don't wait to feel ready. Start, and ready follows.

03

I'm stuck, and there's no one to ask.

THE THOUGHT

I don't want to look stupid in a group. Nobody around me has done this.

THE TRUTH

A doubt left alone for three days becomes a skipped topic. Then a skipped chapter. Then a lost month.

People don't quit because it's hard. They quit because they got stuck alone.

THE FIX

  • One rule: no doubt older than 48 hours
  • Write it down the moment it shows up. Half of them disappear once you phrase them properly.
  • Find your person before you need them: a study partner, a community, a mentor

Asking early isn't weakness. It's how people finish.

04

I have no idea what the questions will look like.

THE THOUGHT

I can read and highlight all day. But what if the exam asks something I've never seen?

THE TRUTH

The exam rarely asks you to define things. It gives you a situation: an athlete, a goal, a limit. Then it asks what you'd do.

Reading more doesn't prepare you for that. Practising does.

THE FIX

  • Do questions before you feel ready
  • Read every explanation, even when you got it right
  • For each question, ask: what is this really asking?

Don't just learn the answers. Learn how to approach the questions.

05

I'm from commerce / arts. How will I clear the CSCS?

THE THOUGHT

Everyone else has a sports science degree. I'm already behind.

THE TRUTH

You're not shut out. CSCS eligibility asks for a bachelor's degree, not a particular one.

And the exam doesn't reward memorising. It rewards reading a situation and making a call. That's a coaching skill, not a degree.

THE FIX

  • Treat the first two weeks as learning a language
  • Terms first, then how things work, then how to apply them
  • Don't compare your Week 1 to someone else's Year 4

Where you started doesn't decide where you finish.

Pick one. Fix it tonight.

You don't need to fix all five. Pick the one that made you uncomfortable while reading. That's usually the one.
